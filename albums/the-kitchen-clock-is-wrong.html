Five minutes before you go?

“Five minutes before you go?” There is a train to catch, work to finish and a good reason to leave. By evening, the unanswered request has become harder to ignore.

The Kitchen Clock Is Wrong follows one ordinary day through a kitchen, a crowded train, an office building and the rooms people return to. Its seven tracks look at the distance that grows when conversation is always saved for later. The person left waiting has her own account of the day, and her own limits.

Two instrumental chapters make room around five songs about borrowed time, public lives and private decisions. A train leaves without its passenger. A name is asked. A chair moves across the floor. These small actions carry the story toward an ending that leaves the conversation open.

Dorado Project brings that domestic story into progressive rock, psychedelic rock and art rock. The album ends at the kitchen table, with an answer that begins by listening.
